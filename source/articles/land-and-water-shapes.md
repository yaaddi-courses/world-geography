# Landlocked countries, peninsulas, archipelagos and straits

**Covers:** Landlocked country, Landlocked countries, Peninsula, Archipelago, Strait

## Where land meets water {#overview}
Open any map and look at the edges of the land. The shape of that edge has shaped history. It decides which countries can trade by sea, where armies could cross and which routes ships use. Four words describe the shapes you will meet most often. Once you know them, you will start spotting them on every map you open.

## Landlocked country {#landlocked-country}
Picture a country whose borders are all with other countries, with no coast on any ocean or open sea. That is a landlocked country. About 44 countries are like this, among them Switzerland, Mongolia, Bolivia, Ethiopia and Kazakhstan.

Why does it matter? Goods leaving or entering the country must pass through a neighbour's ports. That means fees and delays, and a need for the neighbour's goodwill. A few countries are doubly landlocked: every country around them is landlocked too. Uzbekistan and Liechtenstein are the two examples.

Notice that this is a challenge, not a fate. Switzerland is landlocked and still wealthy, thanks to banking, manufacturing and strong rail links.

## Peninsula {#peninsula}
A peninsula is land with water on three sides, joined to a larger landmass on the fourth. Italy is the classic example, a boot reaching into the Mediterranean Sea. Others include Florida, the Korean Peninsula, Iberia, which holds Spain and Portugal, and the Arabian Peninsula, the largest in the world.

A peninsula has a long coastline for its area. For that reason many of its people live near the sea and earn a living from fishing and trade.

## Archipelago {#archipelago}
An archipelago is a group or chain of islands. Japan has about 14,000 islands, although four main ones hold most of the people. The Philippines has more than 7,000. Indonesia is the largest archipelago country in the world, with more than 17,000 islands.

Archipelagos form in different ways. Some, like Hawaii, are the tops of undersea volcanoes. Others are pieces of a continent that were cut off when sea levels rose. With people spread over many islands, ferries and flights become part of daily life.

## Strait {#strait}
A strait is a narrow channel of water that joins two larger bodies of water, usually with land on both sides. Ships use straits as shortcuts, so they matter for trade and for defence.

The Strait of Gibraltar is only about 14 kilometres wide at its narrowest point. It links the Atlantic Ocean to the Mediterranean Sea and separates Spain in Europe from Morocco in Africa. Other well-known straits are the Bosphorus in Turkey, the Strait of Hormuz at the mouth of the Persian Gulf, and the Bering Strait between Alaska and Russia.

## A worked example {#example}
Let's name the shape for each of these places and say why.

| Place | Shape | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Mongolia | Landlocked country | No coast; China and Russia are its neighbours |
| Italy | Peninsula | Water on three sides, joined to Europe in the north |
| Philippines | Archipelago | Thousands of islands in one group |
| Strait of Gibraltar | Strait | A narrow channel joining the Atlantic and the Mediterranean |
| Switzerland | Landlocked country | Surrounded by Germany, France, Italy, Austria and Liechtenstein |

Here is a way to tell a strait from other water. Gibraltar is about 14 kilometres wide, while the Atlantic Ocean is thousands of kilometres across. A strait is small compared with the waters it joins. That size gap is the quickest way to tell it from a bay or a sea.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
A common slip is using "island" and "peninsula" as if they meant the same thing. An island has water on every side. A peninsula is attached to land on one side.

Another slip is calling any narrow stretch of water a strait. A river is not a strait, because a strait connects two seas or oceans.

## Key points {#key-points}
- A landlocked country has no ocean coast, which makes trade harder but does not decide wealth.
- A peninsula has water on three sides and joins land on the fourth.
- An archipelago is a group of islands, such as Japan, the Philippines and Indonesia.
- A strait is a narrow channel joining two larger bodies of water, such as Gibraltar.
