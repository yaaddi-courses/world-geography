# Landlocked countries, peninsulas, archipelagos and straits

**Covers:** Landlocked country, Landlocked countries, Peninsula, Archipelago, Strait

## Where land meets water {#overview}
Maps are mostly about the edge between land and water. The shape of that edge has shaped history: it decides which countries can trade by sea, where armies could cross and which routes ships use. Four words describe the most important shapes, and each one is easy to spot on a map once you know it.

## Landlocked country {#landlocked-country}
A landlocked country has no coast on an ocean or open sea. All of its borders are with other countries. About 44 countries are landlocked, among them Switzerland, Mongolia, Bolivia, Ethiopia and Kazakhstan.

Being landlocked brings real difficulties. Goods must pass through a neighbour's ports, which means fees and delays, and the country depends on its neighbour's goodwill. A few countries are doubly landlocked, which means every country around them is landlocked too. Uzbekistan and Liechtenstein are the two examples.

Some landlocked countries are rich anyway. Switzerland, for instance, has strong banking, manufacturing and rail links. So being landlocked is a challenge, not a fixed fate.

## Peninsula {#peninsula}
A peninsula is a piece of land surrounded by water on three sides and connected to a larger landmass on the fourth. Italy is a famous example, shaped like a boot reaching into the Mediterranean Sea. Others include Florida, the Korean Peninsula, Iberia, which holds Spain and Portugal, and the Arabian Peninsula, the largest in the world.

Peninsulas often have long coastlines compared with their area, so many of their people live near the sea and make a living from fishing and trade.

## Archipelago {#archipelago}
An archipelago is a group or chain of islands. Japan has about 14,000 islands, but four main ones hold most of the population. The Philippines has more than 7,000. Indonesia is the largest archipelago country in the world, with more than 17,000 islands.

Archipelagos can form in different ways. Some are the tops of undersea volcanoes, like Hawaii. Others are pieces of continents that were cut off when sea levels rose. Because the people are spread over many islands, ferries and flights are an important part of life.

## Strait {#strait}
A strait is a narrow channel of water that joins two larger bodies of water. It is often between two pieces of land. Ships use straits as shortcuts, so they are important for trade and also for defence.

The Strait of Gibraltar is only about 14 kilometres wide at its narrowest point. It connects the Atlantic Ocean to the Mediterranean Sea and separates Spain in Europe from Morocco in Africa. Other well-known straits include the Bosphorus in Turkey, the Strait of Hormuz at the mouth of the Persian Gulf, and the Bering Strait between Alaska and Russia.

## A worked example {#example}
Name the shape for each of these places:

| Place | Shape | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Mongolia | Landlocked country | No coast; China and Russia are its neighbours |
| Italy | Peninsula | Water on three sides, joined to Europe in the north |
| Philippines | Archipelago | Thousands of islands in one group |
| Strait of Gibraltar | Strait | A narrow channel joining the Atlantic and the Mediterranean |
| Switzerland | Landlocked country | Surrounded by Germany, France, Italy, Austria and Liechtenstein |

A check of the size gap: Gibraltar is about 14 kilometres wide, and the Atlantic Ocean is thousands of kilometres across. A strait is small compared with the bodies of water it joins. That is the main way to tell it from a bay or a sea.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
Using "island" and "peninsula" as if they meant the same thing. An island is surrounded by water on all sides, while a peninsula is joined to land on one side. Another mistake is calling every narrow stretch of water a strait. A river, for example, is not a strait, because a strait connects two seas or oceans.

## Key points {#key-points}
- A landlocked country has no ocean coast, which makes trade harder but does not decide wealth.
- A peninsula has water on three sides and joins land on the fourth.
- An archipelago is a group of islands, such as Japan, the Philippines and Indonesia.
- A strait is a narrow channel joining two larger bodies of water, such as Gibraltar.
