# Latitude, longitude and the hemispheres

**Covers:** Equator, Hemisphere, Prime meridian, Latitude, Longitude

## A grid on a ball {#overview}
To say where a place is without a picture, people drew an imaginary grid over the globe. Two sets of lines make it work. One set runs around the Earth like rings, and the other runs from pole to pole like the segments of an orange. Every point has two numbers, one from each set. Those two numbers, called coordinates, can find any place from a ship, a plane or a phone.

## Equator {#equator}
The equator is the imaginary line that runs around the middle of the Earth, exactly halfway between the North and South Poles. It is about 40,000 kilometres long. It divides the globe into a northern half and a southern half.

Places on or near it have warm weather all year, because the sun is high in the sky throughout. It passes through countries such as Ecuador, Kenya and Indonesia. Ecuador is even named after it.

## Hemisphere {#hemisphere}
A hemisphere is half of the Earth. There are two common ways to cut it. The equator splits the globe into the Northern and Southern Hemispheres. The prime meridian, together with the line on the opposite side of the world, splits it into the Eastern and Western Hemispheres.

A place can belong to two at once. For example, Canada is in the Northern and Western Hemispheres, while New Zealand is in the Southern and Eastern ones. Seasons are opposite in the two halves: when it is summer in the north, it is winter in the south.

## Prime meridian {#prime-meridian}
The prime meridian is the line that runs from pole to pole through Greenwich, near London, in England. It marks zero degrees of longitude. Countries agreed on it in 1884, so that maps and clocks would use one common starting point.

On the opposite side of the Earth, about halfway round, is the 180 degree line, which runs mostly through the Pacific Ocean. The international date line follows it roughly.

## Latitude {#latitude}
Latitude tells you how far north or south of the equator a place is, measured in degrees. The equator is 0 degrees and the poles are 90 degrees north and 90 degrees south. Lines of latitude are also called parallels, because they never meet.

One degree of latitude is about 111 kilometres on the ground, wherever you are. That makes it easy to estimate north-south distances.

## Longitude {#longitude}
Longitude tells you how far east or west of the prime meridian a place is, from 0 to 180 degrees. Lines of longitude, called meridians, all meet at the poles. Unlike latitude, their spacing changes: a degree of longitude is about 111 kilometres at the equator and shrinks to zero at the poles.

## A worked example {#example}
Read the coordinates of three cities and say which hemispheres they are in:

| City | Latitude | Longitude | Hemispheres |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tokyo | 35.7 degrees N | 139.7 degrees E | Northern, Eastern |
| Nairobi | 1.3 degrees S | 36.8 degrees E | Southern, Eastern |
| Lima | 12.0 degrees S | 77.0 degrees W | Southern, Western |

Now estimate a distance. Tokyo is at 35.7 degrees north and Nairobi at 1.3 degrees south. The difference in latitude is 35.7 + 1.3 = 37 degrees. At about 111 kilometres per degree, the north-south part of the gap is 37 times 111, which is about 4,100 kilometres. The real distance between them is larger because they also differ in longitude.

Notice that Nairobi sits very close to the equator, which matches its position: a latitude of only 1.3 degrees south.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
Mixing up latitude and longitude. A simple reminder is that lines of latitude look like the rungs of a ladder, going across, and give the north-south position. Another mistake is forgetting the direction letters. "36.8" alone could be east or west, and the two places are on opposite sides of the world.

## Key points {#key-points}
- The equator is 0 degrees latitude and splits the globe into the Northern and Southern Hemispheres.
- The prime meridian is 0 degrees longitude, runs through Greenwich and divides east from west.
- Latitude measures north or south, longitude measures east or west, both in degrees.
- One degree of latitude is about 111 kilometres, but a degree of longitude shrinks toward the poles.
