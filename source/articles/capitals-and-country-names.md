# Capital cities and the names of countries

**Covers:** Capital city, DR

## A name is not always what it seems {#overview}
Most of this course asks you to match a country with its capital and flag. Two small points cause many wrong answers. The first is that the capital is often not the largest or best-known city. The second is that a few country names are almost the same, so a short form can mean two different countries. This article clears up both.

## Capital city {#capital-city}
A capital city is the seat of a country's government. It is usually where the head of state and the main government offices work. Often it is also the largest city, as in Paris or Tokyo, but not always. Capitals are sometimes chosen to be a compromise, to be in the centre of the country, or to be new and planned.

Some well-known cases:
- Australia: the capital is Canberra, not Sydney.
- Canada: Ottawa, not Toronto.
- Brazil: Brasilia, not Rio de Janeiro or Sao Paulo.
- Turkey: Ankara, not Istanbul.
- The United States: Washington, D.C., not New York.
- South Africa: three capitals, with Pretoria for the executive, Cape Town for the parliament and Bloemfontein for the courts.

A good habit is to learn the capital together with a fact about it, such as a river or a landmark. That makes it harder to confuse with the biggest city.

## DR {#dr}
The letters DR stand for "Democratic Republic", as in DR Congo. The abbreviation appears on maps, in news reports and in this course.

It matters because two African countries share a similar name. The Democratic Republic of the Congo, usually called DR Congo, has its capital at Kinshasa. The Republic of the Congo, often just called Congo or Congo-Brazzaville, has its capital at Brazzaville. The two capitals face each other across the Congo River, and they are among the closest capital cities in the world.

DR Congo is far larger. It is the second biggest country in Africa by area. Congo is much smaller. If a question says "DR", it points to Kinshasa. If it says "Republic of the Congo", the answer is Brazzaville.

## A worked example {#example}
Use the rule "capital is not always the biggest city" on this short quiz:

| Country | Biggest city (population) | Capital | Same city? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Australia | Sydney | Canberra | No |
| Canada | Toronto | Ottawa | No |
| Brazil | Sao Paulo | Brasilia | No |
| France | Paris | Paris | Yes |
| Japan | Tokyo | Tokyo | Yes |

Out of five countries, three have a capital that is not the biggest city. That makes a lazy guess of "largest city" wrong 60 percent of the time in this sample. The real world has fewer exceptions than that, but enough to cost you marks on a test.

Now the two Congos. Question: "Capital of DR Congo?" The word DR points to Kinshasa. Question: "Capital of the Republic of the Congo?" gives Brazzaville. Reading the short form carefully is the entire trick.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
Answering with the most famous city instead of the capital, such as Sydney for Australia. Another mistake is treating "Congo" and "DR Congo" as one country. When a question gives only "Congo", look at the other clues, such as the flag or the surrounding countries.

## Key points {#key-points}
- A capital city is where the government sits, and it is often not the largest city.
- Learn the capital with a fact that links it to the country.
- DR means Democratic Republic, as in DR Congo with its capital Kinshasa.
- The Republic of the Congo is a different, smaller country with the capital Brazzaville.
