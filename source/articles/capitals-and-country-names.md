# Capital cities and the names of countries

**Covers:** Capital city, DR

## A name is not always what it seems {#overview}
Suppose someone asks you for the capital of Australia. Many people answer Sydney without thinking, because it is the city they have heard of most. That answer is wrong, and the reason it is wrong is worth understanding. In this article we will look at why a capital is often not the biggest city, and then at a second trap: a few country names are so close that a short form can point to two different countries.

## Capital city {#capital-city}
A capital is the city where a country's government does its work. The head of state and the main government offices are usually there. Sometimes the capital is also the largest city, as with Paris or Tokyo. Often it is not, and that is a choice, not a mistake. A country may pick a capital as a compromise between rival cities, put it near the centre of the country, or build a brand new city for the purpose.

Here are some cases you will meet again and again:
- Australia: the capital is Canberra, not Sydney.
- Canada: Ottawa, not Toronto.
- Brazil: Brasilia, not Rio de Janeiro or Sao Paulo.
- Turkey: Ankara, not Istanbul.
- The United States: Washington, D.C., not New York.
- South Africa: three capitals. Pretoria hosts the executive, Cape Town the parliament and Bloemfontein the courts.

Here is a habit that helps. When you learn a capital, attach one fact to it, such as a river it sits on or a landmark. The fact gives your memory a second hook, so the famous big city stops pushing the right answer out.

## DR {#dr}
You will see the letters DR on maps, in the news and in this course. They stand for "Democratic Republic", as in DR Congo.

The letters matter because two neighbouring countries in Africa have almost the same name. The Democratic Republic of the Congo, called DR Congo for short, has its capital at Kinshasa. The Republic of the Congo, often called Congo or Congo-Brazzaville, has its capital at Brazzaville. The two capitals face each other across the Congo River, and they are among the closest pair of national capitals in the world.

The two countries are very different in size. DR Congo is the second biggest country in Africa by area, and Congo is much smaller. So if a question says "DR", think Kinshasa. If it says "Republic of the Congo", think Brazzaville.

## A worked example {#example}
Let's test the idea "the capital is the biggest city" on five countries.

| Country | Biggest city | Capital | Same city? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Australia | Sydney | Canberra | No |
| Canada | Toronto | Ottawa | No |
| Brazil | Sao Paulo | Brasilia | No |
| France | Paris | Paris | Yes |
| Japan | Tokyo | Tokyo | Yes |

Count the rows where the answer is No. There are three out of five. So if you guessed "the largest city" every time, you would be wrong 3 out of 5 times, which is 60 percent, in this sample. The whole world has fewer exceptions than that, but plenty to cost you marks on a test.

Now the two Congos, one question at a time. "What is the capital of DR Congo?" The letters DR tell you it is the larger country, so the answer is Kinshasa. "What is the capital of the Republic of the Congo?" That is the smaller one, so the answer is Brazzaville. Reading the short form slowly is the whole trick.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
A common slip is answering with the most famous city, such as Sydney for Australia, when the question asked for the capital. The fix is to stop and ask yourself, "where does the government sit?", before you answer.

A second slip is treating "Congo" and "DR Congo" as one country. If a question gives you only "Congo", look for other clues, such as the flag or the neighbouring countries on the map.

## Key points {#key-points}
- A capital is where the government sits, and it is often not the largest city.
- Learn each capital together with one fact that ties it to its country.
- DR stands for Democratic Republic, as in DR Congo, whose capital is Kinshasa.
- The Republic of the Congo is a different, smaller country with the capital Brazzaville.
